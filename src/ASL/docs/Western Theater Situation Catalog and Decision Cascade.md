# Western Theater Situation Catalog and Decision Cascade

Status: seventeen reference-only Situation packages imported with deployment and game-map inspection. Command-cascade adaptations and combat remain proposals. Reviewed 2026-10-08.

## Findings and recommendation

Our archive contains the original twenty Panzer Leader Situation Cards. Seventeen are land situations after the D-Day landing cards: the existing St. Lo card and sixteen additional candidates. We have enough material to test substantially different command decisions without inventing every tactical problem.

The five batches now cover all seventeen land Situations, PL04 through PL20. They provide timed attacks, blocking defenses, crossing denial, coalition counterattack, scheduled air support and bridge seizure. That group provides the richest set of contemporaneous command choices, although its units, locations and dates must remain distinct.

Keep Normandy, Lorraine, Market-Garden, Saar, Ardennes and Rhine situations in separate dated campaign contexts. They are not alternative orders available to the same battalion on the same day. Reusing a later card's tactical pattern in Normandy would be an explicitly authored adaptation with a new identity and appropriate forces.

An option that changes a source roster, start time, victory requirement or permission is a **campaign adaptation**. Do not offer it while claiming unchanged legacy fidelity. Options must also be legal for the player's role and available information. No order may create a force or support package that has not been allocated.

Supply is particularly valuable here, but these cards do not provide depot stocks, fuel accounting or delivery throughput. Those must be authored or historically researched separately. Bridge control can enable a supply edge only after route connectivity, clearance, capacity and transit time are satisfied. Controlling a bridge does not instantly refill every unit.

## A concrete Ardennes cascade to prototype

This is an authored command simulation built around the card library, not a claim that these original cards were designed as a linked campaign.

1. **Higher command:** receive multiple dated threats and choose a main defensive priority. Allocate a defined reserve and transport budget among eligible subordinate commands. Author the actual authority and transfer travel times before allowing cross-sector allocation.
2. **Sector commands:** select hold, reinforce or permitted fallback policies. A policy that departs from the source objective creates a named variant. PL13 and PL15 provide distinct defensive problems; PL14 provides a crossing-denial problem.
3. **Local command:** make typed assignments to actual available formations. Engineers committed to PL14 cannot simultaneously clear another route. A reserve moving toward one sector remains in transit until arrival.
4. **Situation execution:** preserve source setup and victory rules in fidelity mode. Campaign variants carry an explicit difference record. Local contacts produce validated ASL Scenario Cards with persistent asset reservations.
5. **Reconciliation:** record crossing state, force survival, time, stocks and penetrations. Update authoritative state once, then deliver knowledge reports under the communication rules.
6. **Later decision windows:** consider PL16, PL17, PL18 and PL19 when their dates, locations, forces and prerequisites permit. They are authored opportunities with their own initialization, not guaranteed rewards unlocked by a simple victory boolean.

Possible typed choices include reserve destination, support package, route, departure window, hold duration and demolition permission. Each option needs prerequisites and visible known costs. Notes remain non-authoritative.

In a historical-start campaign, later source rosters cannot overwrite the current ledger. Choose explicitly between a fresh historical vignette and a persistent adaptation whose roster reflects prior casualties, transfers and replacements. Both are useful modes, but only the latter tests force continuity.

## Original import priorities (now completed)

1. **PL08, Marieulles:** reuses A plus D, provides a ten-turn attack with graded timing and loss assessment. Inspect the source rule's completion-time bands carefully instead of replacing them with a binary victory.
2. **PL16, Bastogne: Prelude:** one board D and a narrower force mix; proves holding objectives and loaded-mortar setup. This requires valid transport relationships, not merely drawing mortar counters over trucks.
3. **PL14, Bulge: Thrust:** C/D adds engineer authority, bridge state and movement deadlines. This is the first strong infrastructure/supply-dependency demonstration.
4. **PL15 and PL13:** provide the competing defensive demands needed to make a higher-level reserve decision meaningful.
5. **PL17, then PL18/19:** add multi-contingent counterattack and scheduled aircraft support when the relevant capabilities are available.
6. **PL09/20 and PL11:** expand engineering and cross-command behavior in their own campaign contexts. Goodwood supplies the second Normandy card; its air and combat execution remain future capabilities.

Relatively simple source rules do not mean the formation gameplay already exists. This survey selects content; it does not certify any card as engine-ready.

## Import record and acceptance gates

For every accepted card, capture source file/hash and PDF page/half, source situation number, displayed title, date as printed, historical-date review status, formations as printed, board transforms, terrain package versions, counter manifest, setup geometry, turn order/length, all victory predicates, special rules, evidence and capability requirements.

Each campaign assignment then adds campaign ID, local number, command owner, world location and confidence, eligibility window, parent mission, persistent roster allocation, resources, dependencies, adaptation record and result contract. Do not derive coordinates from the generalized board artwork.

Before calling an import playable:

- Match all required counter types and counts to source and Imaginative Strategist artwork, with explicit substitutions.
- Review and certify the interpreted A-D terrain, setup boundaries and joins. All four boards now have reference renderings; these are not rules-certified game maps.
- Encode ambiguous or overlapping victory clauses with a reviewed precedence policy rather than guessing at runtime.
- Validate special capabilities, including bridge construction/demolition, transport loading and aircraft scheduling where applicable.
- Test both sides' setups, boundary cases in turn/exit/control scoring, support conflicts and casualty reconciliation.
- Require each tactical decomposition to produce an admissible ASL Scenario Card. One source situation can create several engagements without multiplying assets.

Companion analysis: [Campaign Command, Supply, Leadership and Optional Complexity](<Campaign Command Supply Leadership and Optional Complexity Analysis.md>). This catalog supplies concrete source problems for that analysis and leaves the source material unchanged.

## SS infantry affiliation research, 11 October 2026

Research finding: absence of a dedicated SS infantry row in the Panzer Leader Unit Function Table does not imply absence of SS infantry in its Situations. Infantry role and organizational affiliation are independent. The existing type-level SS tank flag is also insufficient: cards use those printed tank types in forces attributed to Heer formations. This section supersedes any inference that a table designation alone proves the historical affiliation of every instance.

Reviewed the supplied Wikipedia research leads and the archived German Infantry Gruppe reference. They describe squad structure, not a dated scenario order of battle, and cannot determine affiliation. Inspected the original card images for PL05, PL09, PL10, PL14, PL15 and PL18, imported briefings for all seventeen cards, and the archived Osprey Waffen-SS Soldier chronology (printed p.5). The latter corroborates Western deployment broadly, not individual counter allocations.
