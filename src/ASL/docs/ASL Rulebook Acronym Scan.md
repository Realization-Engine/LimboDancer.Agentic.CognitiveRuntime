# ASL Rulebook Acronym Scan

This reference defines the acronyms, initialisms, counter abbreviations, and compact chart or listing codes in the original electronic Advanced Squad Leader rulebook. Entries preserve case, explain alternate meanings, and cite the rules that define or qualify the term. Use the cited rule for the complete procedure and its exceptions.

## Source and citation convention

- Source: `eASLRB_v3_01.pdf`, delivery edition 3.01, 716 physical PDF pages. The credits themselves read Version 3.0, June 2025.
- Verified SHA-256: `957de75be52c34a7de4c20e875d33145e6b7d4ff8f19384c68818e385d41a247`.
- Initial scan and review: 2026-10-25; expanded-term audit: 2026-10-26. The scan covers the complete PDF text, not just the repository conversion of Chapters A to E.
- `p.` and `pp.` mean physical PDF pages, counted from 1. Printed page labels, such as H11 or W13, differ.
- A reference such as `A10.2` identifies the chapter and rule. A range such as `C8.1-.2` preserves the source shorthand for C8.1 through C8.2. Page locators attached to numbered references identify verified rule headings; a rule may continue onto the next page. National vehicle/ordnance notes and named chart keys are cited by name and physical page because they are also sources of definitions.
- The Index and Glossary on pp. 11-42 supplies both definitions and a map of application rules. The numbered references retained below include its secondary applications and exceptions. These are supporting reference trails, not a claim that every cited rule independently spells out the acronym.
- References to Chapters O, P, Q, R, S, T, their CG rules, and module SSRs are reproduced as index locators. This PDF lists those chapters in its contents and index but does not include their rule text. The glossary can establish an expansion; those external module rules cannot be independently verified from this PDF.
- A missing rule number in the index is a search lead. Both the abbreviation and its expanded name must be checked in the rule text, notes, training material, and charts. Where the definition is in an unnumbered introduction or key, the reference names that passage and its physical page.
- Ordinary capitalized rule terms, Roman-numeral model numbers, hex coordinates, counter ID letters, chart case letters, personal initials, and printing artifacts are not acronyms. Compound vehicle Type codes combine the components defined here. Plural forms such as AFVs, MMCs, and SSRs use the singular entry.

| Forms | Distinction |
|---|---|
| `DR` / `dr`; `DRM` / `drm` | Multiple dice versus one die, and the corresponding modifier. |
| `DM` / `dm` | Desperation Morale versus a dismantled weapon. |
| `HT` / `ht` | Heavy Tank versus halftrack. |
| `SP` / `sP`; `SD` / `sD` | Self-propelled versus Smoke Pots; Sand Dune overlay versus Smoke Discharger. |

| `SR`; `RF`; `CS` | Spotting Round or side/rear facing; Rarity Factor or Red Factories; Crew Survival or close-support equipment variant. |

| `NA`; `UK`; `SL` | Not Allowed or North Africa; Unconfirmed Kill or the dotted U.K. national designation; Searchlight or the earlier Squad Leader game. |
| `L`, `H`, `T`, `B`, `F` | Listing column and counter position determine the meaning. See the individual entries. |

| `C#` | Canister Depletion Number in ammo notation; Canister Firepower in the IFT legend. |

## Reading compound and qualified forms

- Vehicle types are compositional. For example `AAtr` combines Anti-Aircraft and truck, `TDht` combines Tank Destroyer and halftrack, and `MTv` combines Medium Tank and variant. The Vehicle Listings Key on p. 338 supplies the components; a national vehicle note can add restrictions.
- A leading weapon-mount code, such as `T75L` or `B75L`, identifies turret or bow/non-turreted mounting followed by caliber and barrel length. It is not a new acronym for each caliber. See D1.8, C2.21, C4.1, and the Vehicle Listings Key on p. 338.
- An ammunition letter followed by a number, such as `A6`, `H7`, or `s8`, combines an ammunition type and a Depletion Number. A superscript can qualify its availability date. See C8.1-C8.9 and the Vehicle Listings Key on p. 338. Vehicular smoke-dispenser Usage Numbers are governed separately by D13.
- A number prefixed to `MC`, `KIA`, or `PAATC` qualifies the result or check; a suffix such as `#` means Number. See A7.301-A7.304, A10.1, A11.6, and the symbol key on p. 42. A #MC is governed by A7.304, and K/# has its own Casualty Reduction procedure in A7.302.
- `CG-LVP`, `FL RFP`, `CC Reaction Fire`, `Non-CC`, `MA AAMG`, and `SW/Gun` combine terms already defined here. Apply the cited rule for the combination rather than treating it as a new independent capability.
- Counter symbols such as concealment `?`, the radioless mark, the unarmored mark, and the leadership-exclusion triangle are graphic notation. Their explanations are in the symbol key on p. 42 and A.10, A12, D1.21, and D14. A text extractor can substitute letters for these symbols.
